For people whose work has lost its edges

The break is for your eyes, your back, and a glass of water. Not for more work.

Anti-Burnout counts the time you actually spend at the screen. Every 20 active minutes it asks for one small, physical thing, and in the evening it tells you the day can end. It never adds a task, never asks a question, and never wants a reply.

A browser tab can't see other apps. Your activity on this page is counted, and while the tab is in the background the app assumes you're working elsewhere.

How it works

1

It counts real active time

Idle time doesn't count. If you've been away for a minute or more, you already took a break: the stretch resets and no card appears.

2

It asks for one small thing

A card appears in the corner every 20 active minutes. The longer the stretch, the more it asks for. The wording rotates so the 4pm card doesn't read like the 10am one.

3

It helps the day end

After 8pm it says the day could reasonably end here. End my day covers the page with a calm screen that counts down to the morning.

The 5-minute reset

One activity at a time, never the same one twice in a row. No forms and no lists. Just a timer and one thing to do.

    Privacy: what it does not do

    • The only input is whether you're active. On this page that means pointer, key and scroll events happened, never which keys or where.
    • No screen reading. No window titles, no screenshots, no clipboard.
    • No network. No accounts, analytics, trackers or third-party fonts. After the page loads, nothing leaves your browser.
    • Only coarse counters are stored: your current stretch, today's total and your settings, in this browser's local storage. Clear them any time in Settings.
    • Whole-computer tracking is opt-in. It uses the browser's Idle Detection permission, which reports only "active" or "idle".

    Prefer something that sits in your menu bar? The desktop app does the same thing system-wide on macOS, Windows and Linux. The evening boundary and the reset come from Off-Duty.
